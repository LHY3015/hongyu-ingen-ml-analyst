## The finding that most surprised me

**The largest single effect of the programme came from two lines of physics rather than from any model.** After the raw and spectral feature matrix plateaued at F1 0.5528, adding two cross-channel features built from the fault mechanism moved it to 0.8225 on the same protocol. That gap of 0.27 is roughly five times the fold-to-fold spread the steadier models carry and twice the distance between the best and worst of the six architectures later trained on the enriched matrix. What surprised me was not that feature engineering helped, but where my instinct had pointed first. Faced with a plateau I went looking at the model, the hyperparameters and the split, and the actual constraint was that a per-channel view cannot express a relation between channels however deep the network is. Slip is one wheel diverging from the other three and stuck is high effort against absent motion, and neither has a signature in any single channel's amplitude or spectrum. Week 7 then found the same two quantities at the top of the attribution ranking for the forest, for the MLP, and, as `torque_max`, carrying 43 % of the gradient in the reinforcement-learning alert head. One representation decision propagated through every model family in the programme.

**The second surprise came from the misses rather than the hits.** Stuck-type faults carry the recall ceiling on this task, caught in 39.5 % of windows against 76.2 % for slip. I had assumed the reason was that a steady-state stuck fault leaves too little for the encoder to find once its onset scrolls out of the buffer. The attention maps say otherwise. In the episode the classifier missed entirely, every one of its 49 windows carries attention selectivity above 1, at a median of 8.7, while the classification head calls all 49 of them normal. The encoder finds the fault span and the head discards it. The stuck-type recall ceiling is therefore a property of the decision layer under the training distribution and not of the representation, which inverts the fix: more capacity in the attention stack addresses a component that is already working, and the recall that remains lives in the pooled readout and the operating point. I want to record the limit of this evidence as carefully as the finding, because it is the kind of result that invites over-reading. The 99 eligible windows tile only two independent episodes and consecutive windows share 49 of their 50 rows, so no comparison between the detected and missed groups is supportable. Only the one-sided claim about the fully missed episode stands, and it happens to be the claim that carries the conclusion.

Both surprises point the same way. Twice I attributed a ceiling to the part of the system I was most able to tune, and twice the constraint sat somewhere I had not instrumented.

## One design decision I would change

**I would fix the selection protocol before running any comparison.** Choosing a configuration on one evaluation unit and discovering later that a fuller one reverses it is a failure that recurs three times in this programme. Each cost an extra round of training, and in two of the three the interim reading had to be withdrawn.

The 1D-CNN's pooling head was chosen on a seven-fold rotation, where the concatenated head measured 0.7894 ± 0.0588. Its optimiser was then chosen on the canonical fold alone at a margin the comparison itself reported as not significant, and once that configuration was rotated across all seven folds it measured 0.7307 ± 0.1147. A decision taken on one fold moved the seven-fold mean by 0.059 and doubled its spread, in a direction the single-fold evidence gave no warning of. In Week 3 the sequence comparison matched the models on width, which left the Transformer with 67.8 K parameters against the GRU's 14.9 K and produced an apparent attention win at F1 0.8289. A parameter-matched control later fell to 0.7604 and became indistinguishable from the GRU at p = 0.76, and the paired test put the original headline within noise of the MLP at p = 0.34. In Week 6 the policy comparison ran on the training layout, where PPO led, while all five held-out layouts reversed the ordering in favour of the group-relative variant.

In all three the model was never the thing under-specified. Equal width is not equal capacity, an unpaired gap over seven folds is noise until it is tested, and a leaderboard computed on the distribution a policy trained on measures memorisation as much as competence. What I would carry into the next programme is a short protocol fixed before the first comparison runs. Name the evaluation unit, whether folds, seeds or layouts, and hold every decision to it. State the capacity control that makes the candidates comparable. Decide the significance test in advance, so that a near-tie is reported as one instead of being resolved by whichever number happens to be larger. The cost of that discipline is a slower first result. The cost of skipping it, measured here, was three reversed conclusions.
